7  Comparing Nonlinear Regression Models

The objective is to compare polynomial regression, regression splines, and kernel methods under a mathematical framework, emphasizing their function spaces, design matrices, dimensionality, and estimation objectives.

1 Common Mathematical Setup

Suppose we observe \(\mathcal D_n = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n\), where \(x_i\in [a, b]\subset \R\),and assume the regression model

\[ Y_i = f_0(x_i) + \epsilon, \quad \E[\epsilon_i\mid X_i]=0, \quad \Var(\epsilon_i\mid X_i) = \sigma^2. \]

Our objective is to estimate the unknown regression function \(f_0(x) = \E[Y\mid X=x]\). Most of the models considered can be written as

\[ f_\beta(x) = \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \phi_j(x) = \boldsymbol\phi(x)^\top \boldsymbol \beta \]

We can define the design matrix \(\boldsymbol\Phi_{n\times p}\):

\[ \boldsymbol \Phi=\begin{bmatrix} \phi_1(x_1) & \ldots & \phi_p(x_1) \\ \vdots && \vdots \\ \phi_1(x_n) & \ldots & \phi_p(x_n) \\ \end{bmatrix} \]

and the corresponding function space is \(\mathcal F = \mathrm{span}\{\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_p\}\). Note that we have three distinctions here:

  • Function space dimension: \(\dim(\mathcal F)= p\) when the basis functions are linearly independent.
  • Design-matrix rank: \(\rank(\boldsymbol \Phi)\leq \min(n, p)\), which is the dimension of the evaluated column space \(\mathcal C(\boldsymbol \phi)\).
  • Effective degrees of freedom: a measure of model flexibility, particularly for penalized splines and kernel methods.

When the design matrix has full column rank, the fitted response is the orthogonal projection:

\[ \hat{\mathbf{y}} = \boldsymbol\Phi(\boldsymbol\Phi^{\top}\boldsymbol\Phi)^{-1}\boldsymbol\Phi^\top \mathbf y = P_{\mathcal C(\boldsymbol{\Phi})}\mathbf y \]

Consequently, for any invertible matrix \(R\in \R^{p\times p}\), \(\mathcal C(\boldsymbol\Phi) = \mathcal C(\boldsymbol \Phi R)\), so the basis transformation \(\boldsymbol \Phi \mapsto \boldsymbol \Phi R\) does not change the OLS fitted values.

2 Polynomial regression

Let \(m\geq 0\) denote the maximum polynomial degree. A polynomal representation can be written as

\[ \mathcal P_m = \left\{f(x) = \sum_{j=0}^m c_j x^j: c_j\in \R \right\} \]

with \(\dim(\mathcal{P}_m) = m+1\).

2.1 Ordinary (monomial) polynomials

Definition 1 \[ f_\beta(x) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \ldots + \beta_m x^m. \]

The basis functions are \(\phi_j(x) = x^j, j=0,\ldots, m\).

The design matrix is the Vandermonde matrix:

\[ \boldsymbol\Phi_{\text{poly}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_1 & x_1^2 & \ldots & x_1^m \\ 1 & x_2 & x_2^2 & \ldots & x_2^m \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots && \vdots \\ 1 & x_n & x_n^2 & \ldots & x_n^m \\ \end{bmatrix} \]

The model has global support: changing any coefficient can affect predictions across the entire domain. High-degree Vandermonde matrices can also be ill-conditioned.

2.2 Bernstein polynomials

In order to use Bernstein polynomials, it’s a good practice to transfrom \(x\in [a, b]\) into: \[ t = \frac{x-a}{b-a} \in [0,1] \]

Definition 2 The Bernstein basis of degree \(m\) consists of

\[ B_{j, m}(t) = \binom{m}{j} t^{j}(1-t)^{m-j},\quad j = 0, \ldots, m \]

The regression function is

\[ f_\beta(x) = \sum_{j=0}^m \beta_j B_{j,m}\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right) \]

The Bernstein design matrix \(X_B\) can be expressed in terms of the ordinary polynomial design matrix \(Z\) as \(X_B = ZR\), where \(R\) is an invertible \((m+1) \times (m+1)\) matrix that depends on the polynomial degree \(m\).

Suppse we observed \((x_i, y_i)\) where \(x_i \in [a,b]\). We then apply the transformation on \(x\):

\[ t_i = \frac{x_i -a}{b-a}. \]

An ordinary polynomial regression of degree \(m\), written in terms of \(t\) is \(f(t) = \sum_{k=0}^m \beta_k t^k\), hence its design matrix

\[ Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_1 & t_1^2 & \ldots & t_1^m \\ 1 & t_2 & t_2^2 & \ldots & t_2^m \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots && \vdots \\ 1 & t_n & t_n^2 & \ldots & t_n^m \\ \end{pmatrix} \]

Equivalently, \(Z_{ik} = t_i^k\). The corresponding regression model is \(\E[Y] = Z\beta\).

We want to express each \(B_{j,m}(t)\) as a linear combination of ordinary \(1, t, \ldots, t^m\). Apply the binomial theorem:

\[ (1-t)^{m-j} = \sum_{r=0}^{m-j} \binom{m-j}{r} (-1)^r t^r. \]

Substitute this into \(B_{j, m} (t)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} B_{j,m}(t) = \binom{m}{j} t^j (1-t)^{m-j} &= \binom{m}{j} t^{j} \sum_{r=0}^{m-j} \binom{m-j}{r} (-1)^r t^r \\ & = \sum_{r=0}^{m-j} (-1)^r \binom{m}{j} \binom{m-j}{r} t^{j+r} \end{aligned} \]

Now let \(k = j+r\). Then

\[ B_{j, m}(t) = \sum_{k=j}^m (-1)^{k-j} \binom{m}{j} \binom{m-j}{k-j} t^k \]

Now we define two basis row vectors:

\[ \begin{aligned} z(t)^\top &= (1, t, t^2, \ldots, t^m), \\ b(t)^\top &= (B_{0, m}(t), B_{1, m}(t), \ldots, B_{m,m}(t)). \end{aligned} \]

We seek \(R\) satisfying \(b(t)^\top = z(t)^\top R\). From the expansion above, the matrix entry in row \(k\), column \(j\), with indices \(0,\ldots, m\) must be

\[ \begin{aligned} R_{kj}=\begin{cases} (-1)^{k-j} \binom{m}{j}\binom{m-j}{k-j}, & k \geq j \\ 0, & k < j \end{cases} \end{aligned} \] Equivalently, using a binomial coefficient identity,

\[ R_{kj} = (-1)^{k-j} \binom{m}{k}\binom{k}{j}, \quad k \geq j \]

So the general transformation matrix has the structure

\[ R = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ -m & m & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \binom{m}{2} & -2 \binom{m}{2} & \binom{m}{2} & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ (-1)^m & (-1)^{m-1}\binom{m}{1} & (-1)^{m-2}\binom{m}{2} & \cdots & 1 \end{pmatrix} \]

Notice that \(R\) is lower triangular with diagonal elements \(R_{jj}= \binom{m}{j}\). Therefore, \(\det(R) = \prod_{j=0}^m \binom{m}{j}\neq 0\). This guarantees that \(R\) is invertible for every nonnegative integer \(m\).

Example 1 For cubic Bernstein regression, the basis functions are

\[ \begin{aligned} B_{0,3}(t) &= \binom{3}{0} t^0 (1-t)^3 = 1-3t+3t^2 -t^3 \\ B_{1,3}(t) &= \binom{3}{1} t^1 (1-t)^2 = 3t(1-2t+t^2) = 3t -6t^2 + 3t^3 \\ B_{2,3}(t) &= \binom{3}{2} t^2 (1-t)^1 = 3t^2(1-t) = 3t^2 - 3t^3 \\ B_{3,3}(t) &= \binom{3}{3} t^3 (1-t)^0 = t^3 \end{aligned} \]

The corresponding transformation is

\[ \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}B_{0,3}(t) & B_{1,3}(t) & B_{2,3}(t) & B_{3,3}\end{pmatrix}}_{b(t)^\top} = \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&t&t^2&t^3\end{pmatrix}}_{z(t)^\top} \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0 \\ -3 & 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & -6 & -3 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ \end{pmatrix}}_{R} \]

For \(n\) observations, this becomes:

\[ X_B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_1 & t_1^2 & t_1^3 \\ 1& t_2 & t_2^2 & t_2^3 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & t_n & t_n^2 & t_n^3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0 \\ -3 & 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & -6 & -3 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ \end{pmatrix} \]

The Bernstein basis satisfies

\[ B_{j,m}(t) \geq 0, \quad \sum_{j=0}^m B_{j, m}(t) = 1. \]

It provides a useful representation for shape-constrained regression, such as imposing monotonicity through restrictions on coefficients. Without such restrictions, however, the basis spans exactly \(\mathcal P_m\).

2.3 Orthogonal polynomials

Definition 3 We construct polynomials \(q_0, \ldots, q_m\), where \(\deg(q_j)=j\), that are orthogonal under a specified inner product. For empirical orthonormal polynomials, we define

\[ \langle g, h \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n g(x_i) h(x_i), \quad \text{s.t. } \langle q_j, q_k\rangle_n = \delta_{jk} = \begin{cases}1, & j=k \\ 0, & j\neq k\end{cases} \]

The regression function then is:

\[ f_\beta(x) = \sum_{j=0}^m \beta_j q_j(x). \]

Example 2 Consider the cubic polynomial basis \(\mathcal P_3=\mathrm{span}\{1,x,x^2, x^3\}\). We want to construct four polynoimal \(q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3\) such that

\[ \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n q_j(x_i)q_k(x_i) = \delta_{jk} \]

Starting from the Vandermonde matrix,

\[ \frac{1}{n} V^\top V = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf 1^\top \mathbf 1 & \mathbf 1^\top \mathbf x& \mathbf 1^\top\mathbf x^2 &\mathbf 1^\top \mathbf x^3 \\ \mathbf x^\top \mathbf 1 & \mathbf x^\top \mathbf x & \mathbf x^\top \mathbf x^2 & \mathbf x^\top \mathbf x^3 \\ (\mathbf x^2)^\top \mathbf 1 & (\mathbf x^2)^\top \mathbf x & (\mathbf x^2)^\top \mathbf x^2 & (\mathbf x^2)^\top \mathbf x^3 \\ (\mathbf x^3)^\top \mathbf 1 & (\mathbf x^3)^\top \mathbf x & (\mathbf x^3)^\top \mathbf x^2 & (\mathbf x^3)^\top \mathbf x^3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} n & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i& \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 &\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^3 \\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^3 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4 \\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^3 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^5 \\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^3 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4& \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^5 & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^6 \end{pmatrix} \]

Now we want to find an upper triangular matrix \(U\) such that

\[ \frac{1}{n}V^\top V = U^\top U \]

By calculation, we can obtain

\[ U = \begin{pmatrix} 1 &\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i & \frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^2 & \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^3 \\ 0& \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^2 -(\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i)^2} & \frac{\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^3 - (\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i)(\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^2)}{u_{11}} &\frac{\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^4 - (\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i)(\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^3)}{u_{11}} \\ 0&0&\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^4 - (\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^2)^2 - u^2_{12}} & \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^5 - (\frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^2)(\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^3)- u_{12}u_{13}}{u_{22}}\\ 0&0&0& \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^6 - \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum x_i^3 \right)^2 - u_{13}^2 - u_{23}^2} \end{pmatrix} \]

The full-rank transformation matrix \(R\) is

\[ R = U^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1& -\frac{u_{01}}{u_{11}}& \frac{u_{01}u_{12}-u_{02}u_{11}}{u_{11}u_{22}}& \frac{-u_{01}u_{12}u_{23}+u_{01}u_{13}u_{22}+u_{02}u_{11}u_{23}-u_{03}u_{11}u_{22}}{u_{11}u_{22}u_{33}} \\ 0& \frac1{u_{11}}& -\frac{u_{12}}{u_{11}u_{22}}& \frac{u_{12}u_{23}-u_{13}u_{22}}{u_{11}u_{22}u_{33}} \\ 0&0& \frac1{u_{22}}& -\frac{u_{23}}{u_{22}u_{33}} \\ 0&0&0& \frac1{u_{33}} \end{pmatrix}. \]

The orthonomal polynomial functions should be

\[ Z = VR = (\mathbf q_0, \mathbf q_1, \mathbf q_2, \mathbf q_3) \]

where

\[ \begin{aligned} q_0(x) &= 1\\ q_1(x) &= -\frac{u_{01}}{u_{11}} + \frac{x}{u_{11}}\\ q_2(x) &= \frac{u_{01}u_{12} - u_{02}u_{11}}{u_{11}{u_{22}}} - \frac{u_{12}}{u_{11}u_{22}}x + \frac{x^2}{u_{22}}\\ q_3(x) &= \frac{-u_{01}u_{12}u_{23}+u_{01}u_{13}u_{22}+u_{02}u_{11}u_{23}-u_{03}u_{11}u_{22}}{u_{11}u_{22}u_{33}} + \frac{u_{12}u_{23}-u_{13}u_{22}}{u_{11}u_{22}u_{33}}x -\frac{u_{23}}{u_{22}u_{33}}x^2 +\frac{x^3}{u_{33}} \end{aligned} \]

\[ \boldsymbol \Phi_{\text{Bern}} = \boldsymbol\Phi_{\text{poly}}R_B, \quad \boldsymbol \Phi_{\text{orth}} = \boldsymbol\Phi_{\text{poly}}R_O \]

Therefore,

\[ \mathcal C(\boldsymbol \Phi_{\text{poly}}) = \mathcal C(\boldsymbol \Phi_{\text{Bern}}) = \mathcal (\boldsymbol \Phi_{\text{orth}}) \]

and their unpenalized OLS fitted values are identical in exact arithmetic.

4 Kernel Methods

Definition 4 Let \(c_1, \ldots, c_M\) denote \(M\) selected centers, and let \(h>0\) be a bandwidth parameter. We define the kernel basis functions as

\[ \phi_j(x) = K\left(\frac{x-c_j}{h}\right), \quad j=1,\ldots , M. \]

A common choice is the Gaussian kernel,

\[ K(u) = \exp\left(\frac{-u^2}{2}\right), \quad \phi_j(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{(x-c_j)^2}{2h^2}\right) \]

The regression function is

\[ f_\beta(x) = \beta_0 +\sum_{j=1}^M \beta_j\phi_j(x). \]

def row_tensor(bases):
    out = bases[0]
    for mat in bases[1:]:
        out = (out[:, :, None] * mat[:, None, :]).reshape(len(out), -1)
    return out 

class OLSBasisModel:
    @staticmethod
    def _array(X):
        X = np.asarray(X, dtype=float)
        if X.ndim == 1:
            X = X.reshape(-1, 1)
        if X.ndim != 2 or len(X) == 0:
            raise ValueError("X must have shape (n,) or (n, d).")
        return X
    
    def fit(self, X, y):
        X = self._array(X)
        y = np.asarray(y, dtype=float).reshape(-1)

        if len(X) != len(y):
            raise ValueError("X and y must have the same length.")
        self.n_features_in_ = X.shape[1]

        self._setup(X)
        Phi = self._basis(X)
        self.coef_, _, self.df_, _ = np.linalg.lstsq(Phi, y, rcond=None)
        self.condition_number_ = np.linalg.cond(Phi)
        return self

    def design_matrix(self, X):
        if not hasattr(self, "coef_"):
            raise RuntimeError("Fit the model first.")
        X = self._array(X)
        if X.shape[1] != self.n_features_in_:
            raise ValueError("Incorrect number of features.")
        return self._basis(X)
    
    def predict(self, X):
        Phi = self.design_matrix(X)
        return Phi @ self.coef_

class Polynomial(OLSBasisModel):
    def __init__(self, degree=3, kind="monomial"):
        if kind not in ("monomial", "bernstein", "orthogonal"):
            raise ValueError("Invalid polynomial basis.")

        self.degree = degree
        self.kind = kind
    
    def _setup(self, X):
        # Scale Bernstein and orthogonal polynomials.
        if self.kind != "monomial":
            self.bounds_ = np.column_stack((X.min(axis=0), X.max(axis=0)))
            if np.any(self.bounds_[:, 1] == self.bounds_[:, 0]):
                raise ValueError("Predictors must vary.")

        # Empirical orthogonalization.
        if self.kind == "orthogonal":
            V = self._raw_basis(X)
            if len(X) < V.shape[1] or np.linalg.matrix_rank(V) < V.shape[1]:
                raise ValueError("Polynomial design is rank deficient.")
            _, R = np.linalg.qr(V, mode="reduced")
            self.R_ = R / np.sqrt(len(X))
    
    def _raw_basis(self, X):
        bases = []
        for k in range(X.shape[1]):
            x = X[:, k]
            if self.kind == "monomial":
                B = x[:, None] ** np.arange(self.degree + 1)
            else: 
                a, b = self.bounds_[k]
                t = (x - a) / (b - a)

                if self.kind == "bernstein":
                    m = self.degree
                    B = np.column_stack(
                        [comb(m, j) * t**j * (1 - t)**(m - j) for j in range(m + 1)]
                    )
                else:
                    B = np.polynomial.legendre.legvander(2 * t - 1, self.degree)
            bases.append(B)
        return row_tensor(bases)

    def _basis(self, X):
        V = self._raw_basis(X)
        if self.kind == "orthogonal":
            return np.linalg.solve(self.R_.T, V.T).T
        return V

class Spline(OLSBasisModel):
    def __init__(self, kind="linear", n_knots=4):
        if kind not in ("linear", "cubic", "natural", "restricted"):
            raise ValueError("Invalid spline kind.")

        self.kind = "natural" if kind == "restricted" else kind
        self.n_knots = n_knots

    def _setup(self, X):
        self.bounds_ = np.column_stack((X.min(axis=0), X.max(axis=0)))

        self.knots_ = []

        for j in range(X.shape[1]):
            x = X[:, j]

            q = np.arange(1, self.n_knots + 1) / (self.n_knots + 1)

            knots = np.quantile(x, q) if self.n_knots > 0 else np.array([])

            a, b = self.bounds_[j]

            if a == b or np.any(np.diff(np.r_[a, knots, b]) <= 0):
                raise ValueError("Knots are not distinct; use fewer knots.")
            self.knots_.append(knots)

    def _basis(self, X):
        bases = []

        for j in range(X.shape[1]):
            x = X[:, j]
            inner = self.knots_[j]

            cols = [np.ones_like(x), x]

            if self.kind == "linear":
                cols += [np.maximum(x - t, 0) for t in inner]

            elif self.kind == "cubic":
                cols += [x**2, x**3]
                cols += [np.maximum(x - t, 0)**3 for t in inner]

            else:  # Natural / restricted cubic
                a, b = self.bounds_[j]

                if len(inner) > 0:
                    last = inner[-1]

                    for t in np.r_[a, inner[:-1]]:
                        h = (
                            np.maximum(x - t, 0)**3
                            - (b - t) / (b - last) * np.maximum(x - last, 0)**3
                            + (last - t) / (b - last) * np.maximum(x - b, 0)**3
                        )

                        cols.append(h)

            bases.append(np.column_stack(cols))

        return row_tensor(bases)

class Kernels(OLSBasisModel):
    def __init__(self, n_centers=5, bandwidth=None, centers=None):
        self.n_centers = n_centers
        self.bandwidth = bandwidth
        self.centers = centers

    def _setup(self, X):
        d = X.shape[1]

        # Automatically select evenly spaced centers
        if self.centers is None:
            grids = [np.linspace(X[:, j].min(), X[:, j].max(), self.n_centers) for j in range(d)]
            self.centers_ = np.asarray(list(product(*grids)))
        else:
            C = np.asarray(self.centers, dtype=float)
            self.centers_ = C.reshape(-1, 1) if C.ndim == 1 else C

            if self.centers_.shape[1] != d:
                raise ValueError("Center dimension does not match X.")

        # Default bandwidth based on predictor range
        span = np.mean(np.ptp(X, axis=0))

        self.bandwidth_ = (
            span / max(self.n_centers - 1, 1)
            if self.bandwidth is None
            else float(self.bandwidth)
        )

        if self.bandwidth_ <= 0:
            raise ValueError("Bandwidth must be positive.")

    def _basis(self, X):
        sqdist = np.sum((X[:, None, :] - self.centers_[None, :, :]) ** 2, axis=2)

        return np.column_stack([np.ones(len(X)), np.exp(-sqdist / (2 * self.bandwidth_**2))])

class SurfaceGenerator:
    SURFACES = (
        "pringle", "monkey_saddle", "ripple", "rastrigin", "diagonal_ridge",
        "interaction", "discontinuous_step",
    )

    def __init__(self, surface="pringle", domain=(-1.0, 1.0), noise_std=0.2,
                 standardize=True, seed=6400, ridge_width=0.18,
                 ripple_frequency=4 * np.pi):
        surface = surface.lower().strip().replace(" ", "_")

        if surface not in self.SURFACES:
            raise ValueError(f"Unknown surface: {surface}")
        lo, hi = map(float, domain)
        if not lo < hi:
            raise ValueError("Domain must satisfy lower < upper.")

        if noise_std < 0 or ridge_width <= 0 or ripple_frequency <= 0:
            raise ValueError("Invalid noise or surface parameters.")

        self.surface = surface
        self.domain = (lo, hi)
        self.noise_std = float(noise_std)
        self.standardize = standardize
        self.ridge_width = ridge_width
        self.ripple_frequency = ripple_frequency
        self.rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)

        # Approximate population mean and standard deviation
        self.mean_, self.scale_ = 0.0, 1.0

        if standardize:
            u = np.linspace(lo, hi, 201)
            U, V = np.meshgrid(u, u)
            X_ref = np.column_stack([U.ravel(), V.ravel()])
            z_ref = self._raw_surface(X_ref)
            self.mean_ = float(z_ref.mean())
            self.scale_ = float(z_ref.std())
            if self.scale_ < 1e-12:
                raise ValueError("Surface is nearly constant.")

    @staticmethod
    def _as_points(X):
        X = np.asarray(X, dtype=float)
        if X.ndim == 1 and X.shape == (2,):
            X = X.reshape(1, 2)
        if X.ndim != 2 or X.shape[1] != 2:
            raise ValueError("X must have shape (n, 2).")
        return X

    # Define the true regression surfaces
    def _raw_surface(self, X):
        x, y = X[:, 0], X[:, 1]
        if self.surface == "pringle":
            return x**2 - y**2
        if self.surface == "monkey_saddle":
            return x**3 - 3 * x * y**2
        if self.surface == "ripple":
            r = np.hypot(x, y)
            return np.exp(-r**2) * np.cos(self.ripple_frequency * r)
        if self.surface == "rastrigin":
            return 20 + x**2 + y**2 - 10 * np.cos(2 * np.pi * x) - 10 * np.cos(2 * np.pi * y)
        if self.surface == "diagonal_ridge":
            return np.exp(-(x - y)**2 / (2 * self.ridge_width**2))
        if self.surface == "interaction":
            return np.sin(np.pi * x * y) + 0.3 * x * y

        return np.where(x + y >= 0, 1.0, -1.0)  # Discontinuous step

    # Evaluate the noiseless regression function
    def true_function(self, X):
        X = self._as_points(X)
        z = self._raw_surface(X)
        return (z - self.mean_) / self.scale_

    # Generate observations with Gaussian noise
    def sample(self, n, seed=None):
        if not isinstance(n, (int, np.integer)) or n <= 0:
            raise ValueError("n must be a positive integer.")

        rng = np.random.default_rng(seed) if seed is not None else self.rng
        lo, hi = self.domain
        X = rng.uniform(lo, hi, size=(n, 2))
        y_true = self.true_function(X)
        epsilon = rng.normal(loc=0, scale=self.noise_std, size=n)
        y = y_true + epsilon
        return X, y, y_true

    # Create a regular grid for visualization.
    def grid(self, resolution=120):
        if resolution < 2:
            raise ValueError("Resolution must be at least 2.")

        lo, hi = self.domain
        u = np.linspace(lo, hi, resolution)
        U, V = np.meshgrid(u, u)
        X_grid = np.column_stack([U.ravel(), V.ravel()])
        Z = self.true_function(X_grid).reshape(U.shape)
        return U, V, Z

    # Visualize the prediction surface
    def visualize(self, resolution=120, X=None, y=None):
        U, V, Z = self.grid(resolution)
        fig = plt.figure(figsize=(12, 4.5))
        # 3D prediction surface.
        ax1 = fig.add_subplot(1, 2, 1, projection="3d")
        ax1.plot_surface(U, V, Z, cmap="viridis", linewidth=0, antialiased=True)

        ax1.set(xlabel="x1", ylabel="x2", zlabel="f(x1, x2)", title="True surface")
        ax1.view_init(elev=25, azim=-60)

        # 2D contour visualization.
        ax2 = fig.add_subplot(1, 2, 2)
        contour = ax2.contourf(U, V, Z, levels=25, cmap="viridis")
        fig.colorbar(contour, ax=ax2, label="f(x1, x2)")
        ax2.set(xlabel="x1", ylabel="x2", title="Contour map", aspect="equal")

        # Overlay sampled observations.
        if X is not None:
            X = self._as_points(X)
            ax2.scatter(X[:, 0], X[:, 1], s=10, c="black", alpha=0.4)

            if y is not None:
                y = np.asarray(y).reshape(-1)
                if len(y) != len(X):
                    raise ValueError("X and y must match.")
                ax1.scatter(X[:, 0], X[:, 1], y, s=8, c="crimson", alpha=0.45)
        fig.suptitle(self.surface.replace("_", " ").title())
        fig.tight_layout()
        plt.show()
        return fig, (ax1, ax2)

def regression_metrics(y_true, y_pred, prefix="test"):
    y_true = np.asarray(y_true)
    y_pred = np.asarray(y_pred)
    residuals = y_true - y_pred
    mse = np.mean(residuals**2)
    mae = np.mean(np.abs(residuals))
    rmse = np.sqrt(mse)
    ss_res = np.sum(residuals**2)
    ss_tot = np.sum((y_true - y_true.mean())**2)
    r2 = 1 - ss_res / ss_tot if ss_tot > 0 else np.nan
    return {f"{prefix}_MSE": mse, f"{prefix}_RMSE": rmse,
            f"{prefix}_MAE": mae, f"{prefix}_R2": r2}

The condition number measures the numerical sensitivity of the design matrix,

\[ \kappa(\Phi)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}(\Phi)}{\sigma_{\min}(\Phi)}. \]

A value near \(1\) indicates a well-conditioned design matrix. A large value indicates that some columns are nearly linearly dependent, so small numerical or data changes can produce large changes in the estimated coefficients. It describes numerical stability rather than predictive accuracy.
